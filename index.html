The Acceleration Question

Something in AI is measurably speeding up. Much of what is said about it is hype, fear, or guesswork. This report separates the two, so you can see what we know, what we only infer, and what nobody knows.

Not a prediction, not an advertisement, not a warning. Every claim is tagged by how well it is supported. Written in English (AU); units metric; dates YYYY-MM-DD.

Executive summary (readable on its own — about 1 minute)

  • BLUE · FACT Measured AI capability, spending and use are rising fast: training compute of notable models has grown ~4.1× per year and of frontier models ~5.3× per year (to May 2024) [S4]; the length of software tasks AI can finish autonomously doubled roughly every 6.5 months over 2019–2025 and every ~4.3 months since 2023 [S2][S3]; global corporate AI investment reached $581.7 billion in 2025 [S1].
  • BLUE · FACT The progress is jagged: models that win maths-olympiad gold read analogue clocks correctly about half the time [S1].
  • RED · RISK Harms are already measurable: US fraud complaints with an AI reference reported adjusted losses of about $893 million in 2025, and the FBI notes many victims do not realise AI was involved [S8]; entry-level software employment (age 22–25) fell nearly 20% since 2024 [S1].
  • OCHRE · SPECULATION Whether this becomes a civilisation-scale transformation, a plateau, or something messier is not known. Cyclic “fourth wave” history theories are contested metaphors, not forecasts [S13]. Radical life extension is speculation; there is no evidence it is expected.
  • GREEN · ACTION One person can help ordinary people understand this by publishing small, sourced, honestly-labelled pages — starting with this one, after human review.

1The Moment

Most people met modern AI as a chat box. Behind that box, three things are happening at once: models are getting more capable, they are getting cheaper to use, and enormous physical infrastructure is being built to run them. Each could stall for its own reasons. Together they make the question worth asking: is this the start of a change as large as printing, electricity or the internet — or a bubble of impressive demos?

PURPLE · INTERPRETATION

This report’s working answer: the measured trends justify taking the question seriously; they do not justify certainty in either direction. The honest position is a wide range of outcomes.

RED · CHALLENGE TO THE THESIS
  • “Exponential-looking” is not “sustained exponential”. Most growth curves bend into S-curves when a resource runs out (power, chips, data, money, attention).
  • Benchmarks are not the economy. A model passing an exam is not a workplace transformed.
  • “Unprecedented” is a claim to test, not assume: every earlier wave also felt unprecedented to people living through it.
  • Much of the loudest evidence comes from organisations that sell, or invest in, AI.

2What Is Actually Changing?

Six measurements, each with its date and limits. Source numbers in brackets link to §11.

BLUE · FACT
4.1–5.3× / yr

Training compute

Epoch AI, 2010–May 2024: notable models 4.1× per year (90% CI 3.7–4.6×); frontier (top-10) models 5.3× (4.9–5.7×). Recent frontier growth is 3.3–4.2× depending on method; Epoch cannot rule out acceleration. Database not comprehensive. [S4]

BLUE · FACT
$581.7 bn

Corporate AI investment, 2025

Up 130% on the prior year (Stanford AI Index 2026, published 2026-04-13). AI data-centre power capacity: 29.6 GW. [S1]

BLUE · FACT
53%

Population adoption in 3 years

Generative AI reached 53% population adoption within three years (AI Index); the US ranks 24th at 28.3%. Definitions vary between countries and technologies. [S1]

BLUE · FACT
9×–900× / yr

Falling price of a given capability

Epoch (2025-03, six benchmarks, data to 2025-02): price to reach a given performance fell 9–900× per year depending on milestone (40× for GPT-4-level PhD science questions); the fastest declines were the latest and may not persist. An independent paper (2025-11) finds ~5–10× per year. [S5][S6]

BLUE · FACT
88%

Organisational adoption

AI Index 2026: organisational adoption of AI reached 88%. Definitions of “adoption” vary by survey. [S1]

The frontier is jagged, not smooth

Technical note: how these numbers were handled

METR’s “50% time horizon” is the human-completion time of tasks that an AI agent solves half the time. The trend is robust to a 10× measurement error in absolute terms (shifting predicted arrival of month-long tasks by about 2 years), but METR flags external validity — whether benchmark tasks resemble real work — as the main uncertainty [S2]. Epoch’s price-decline range (9–900× per year) depends on benchmark and performance milestone, and covers models up to 2025-02 [S5]; the independent paper attributes ~3× per year to algorithmic efficiency alone [S6]. Epoch’s compute estimates are sensitive to modelling choices (e.g. whether the AlphaGo outliers are excluded), which is why recent frontier growth is 3.3× or 4.2× depending on method [S4]. A 2026-01 METR revision changed some model estimates substantially (older GPT-4 versions down 35–57%, GPT-5 up 55%), which steepens the recent trend [S3].

3Why Scale Matters

A tool used by a hundred people is a product. A tool used by a billion is infrastructure — and infrastructure changes what is normal, what is cheap, and what can go wrong. The same system behaves differently at each order of magnitude.

PURPLE · INTERPRETATION

10² – 10³ users or agents

Lab pilots. Failures are anecdotes; a human checks everything.

PURPLE · INTERPRETATION

10⁴ – 10⁶

Rare failures become daily events. Workflows reorganise around the tool. Misuse starts to pay.

PURPLE · INTERPRETATION

10⁷ – 10⁸

Habits, norms and regulation lag behind. Energy and chip supply become strategic constraints.

PURPLE · INTERPRETATION

10⁹ + (and agents ≫ people)

Agents interacting with agents; failures correlate across everything that shares a model.

Scaling by building: the compute race

BLUE · FACT The AI Index reports AI data-centre power capacity of 29.6 GW and corporate investment of $581.7 billion in 2025 [S1]. The IEA’s Energy and AI base case puts global data-centre electricity use at about 415 TWh in 2024 (~1.5% of world electricity) and about 945 TWh in 2030 (just under 3%); electricity use of accelerated (AI) servers grows ~30% per year, and the US adds ~240 TWh (+130%). The IEA states “substantial uncertainty”: its 2035 sensitivity cases range from a plateau near 700 TWh (Headwinds) to over 1,700 TWh (Lift-Off) [S7].

OCHRE · CONTESTED xAI (Elon Musk) is the most publicised example of a “build it bigger, build it faster” approach: Colossus in Memphis, reportedly powered partly by on-site gas turbines and batteries, with a stated aim of gigawatt-scale clusters. Reported GPU counts and power (1 GW vs 2 GW; ~555,000 vs ~770,000 GPUs) differ between sources, are largely company claims, and one analysis of satellite imagery argues the site is well short of 1 GW [S14]. I treat the direction (single-site clusters at gigawatt scale are being attempted) as plausible and the numbers as unverified. Other major labs pursue comparable buildouts; this report does not rank them.

The fourth-wave hypothesis

The operator’s intuition resembles the Strauss–Howe generational theory: history moves in roughly 80–100-year cycles of four “turnings”, the fourth being a crisis followed by renewal [S13].

GREEN · WHERE THE ANALOGY HELPS
  • Technologies do arrive in waves: a breakthrough, a build-out, a shake-out, then ordinary life.
  • Institutions typically lag a new technology by decades — a useful prompt to ask “what lags here?”.
  • Generations do experience formative events differently.
RED · WHERE IT FAILS
  • Critics say it lacks empirical rigour and cannot be falsified; I found no systematic empirical test [S13].
  • It assumes outlooks are fixed by birth era; studies show beliefs change over a lifetime.
  • It is US-centred and picks dates to fit; AI diffusion is a global, technology-driven process, not a generational calendar.
OCHRE · SPECULATION

Treat “fourth wave” as a metaphor for diffusion (S-curves, build-out, backlash, normalisation), not as a schedule. A stronger version of the idea would state in advance what observation would prove it wrong. This one does not.

5Possible Human Futures

Possibility is not probability. Each item separates what is observed from what is imagined.

GREEN · OPPORTUNITY

Medicine

BLUE Physicians reported up to 83% less time writing notes; but only 5% of reviewed clinical-AI studies used real clinical data [S1]. OCHRE Large health gains remain unproven.

GREEN · OPPORTUNITY

Science

BLUE AI-related publications in natural, physical and life sciences rose 26–28% year on year [S1] (a count of papers, not of discoveries).

GREEN · OPPORTUNITY

Education

BLUE Four in five US high-school and college students use AI for schoolwork, but only 6% of teachers say school policies are clear [S1]. PURPLE The opportunity is personalised help; the gap is guidance.

OCHRE · SPECULATION

Creativity, community, relationships

BLUE About half of Americans expect AI to worsen creative thinking and relationships (Pew, June 2025) [S9]. Benefits (new tools, accessibility, shared projects) are plausible but not measured here.

Longevity and immortality — kept deliberately separate

BLUE · ESTABLISHED

Life expectancy is not guaranteed to rise: WHO reports global life expectancy fell by 1.8 years between 2019 and 2021 (pandemic) [S10], and its 2026-10-02 estimates put it back at 73.3 years in 2023 versus 73.4 in 2019, with healthy life expectancy (62.8 years) still 0.4 years below 2019 [S10]. Established gains come from public health, vaccines, treatments and prevention — not from a single breakthrough.

OCHRE · SPECULATION

Radical life extension, “digital continuity” and indefinite survival are hypotheses. This report found no evidence in this research pass that they are expected, scheduled or near. AI may speed drug discovery or diagnosis; that is not the same as ending ageing.

RED · RISKS WITH WEAK DATA HERE

Surveillance, concentrated power, security (AI-assisted cyber-attack), misinformation and unequal access are well-argued concerns but I did not assemble quantified evidence for them in this pass. Treat them as open risks, not measured ones — and note that “synthetic slop” (low-quality generated content) is widely discussed but was not measured here.

PURPLE · INTERPRETATION

The two lists in §5 and §6 are not opposites of one outcome. The same capability that cuts clinicians’ paperwork also makes a convincing cloned voice cheap. Dual-use is the rule.

8Making This Understandable

PURPLE · INTERPRETATION Why many people may not see this: (1) the change is mostly invisible infrastructure; (2) the frontier is jagged, so personal experience varies; (3) hype fatigue — every wave arrives wrapped in promotion; (4) experts and the public are shown to differ in outlook (optimistic 59%, nervous 52% in one AI Index summary [S1]). These are hypotheses about audiences, not measured findings.

State the finding, then its evidence class, before any story.

GREEN · DESIGN RULE

Show the uncertainty

Ranges, “unknown” rows, and a challenge-the-thesis box in every report.

GREEN · DESIGN RULE

One idea per card

Plain language first; technical detail in expandable sections.

GREEN · DESIGN RULE

Work offline, no trackers

A single file anyone can save, print, translate and forward. (A web host may still log visitors’ IP addresses; GitHub says it does [S11].)
